# Applying two passages to a fictional rehearsal problem

**Problem:** a fictional rehearsal leader wants to surface uncertain entrances
and questions from participants. **Source:** the complete authored fixture
[A rehearsal experiment, revision 1](fictional-source.md). It is an illustrative
account, not empirical evidence that an intervention will succeed elsewhere.

## R1 — Try a short silent read before the unfamiliar passage

- **Passage locator:** fictional source revision 1, assigned paragraph
  [P1](fictional-source.md#p1).
- **Source claim:** in the described session, silent reading preceded the
  group's identification of two uncertain entrances. The account does not
  measure later performance or establish that silent reading caused this.
- **Applied inference:** try a brief silent read and ask participants to mark
  uncertain entrances before discussing them. This applies the account's
  sequence to the leader's entrance problem; it is a proposed trial.
- **Assumptions/limits:** participants can access/read the score and the leader
  has time for this step. Check whether uncertain entrances are identified;
  do not promise better singing or treat the anecdote as a controlled study.

## R2 — Offer an anonymous question option after rehearsal

- **Passage locator:** fictional source revision 1, assigned paragraph
  [P2](fictional-source.md#p2).
- **Source claim:** anonymous written questions surfaced three timing questions
  that had not been voiced in the group. The account supplies no reason for
  the silence and no comparison with other collection methods.
- **Applied inference:** offer an optional anonymous question channel after
  rehearsal and inspect whether it surfaces additional timing questions. The
  channel is proposed for this leader's question-discovery problem; it does
  not diagnose participants' confidence or motivation.
- **Assumptions/limits:** the channel is accessible and the leader can preserve
  anonymity. Compare question topics with those already voiced; do not claim
  that any difference proves a particular psychological explanation.

**Timing:** now, R1; after the same rehearsal, R2. These are references to the
two evidence records, not additional recommendations.
